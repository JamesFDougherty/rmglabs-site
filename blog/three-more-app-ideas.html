Three More App Ideas: Day-End Offers, VM Owners and Home Upkeep

By RMG LabsNovember 26, 20263 min read

Some of the most useful apps do one unglamorous job well. Here are three more, following our earlier list of four app ideas worth building: one for a food business, one for an IT team and one for the house. These are ideas only. None of them has been built, and nothing below describes a product you can download or buy.

Idea 1: A Day-End Offer List for Food Shops

Who it helps: bakeries, coffee shops and delis that often have good food left an hour before closing, and the regulars who would happily take it home.

Core features:

  • A screen where staff post what is left in a few taps: item, quantity, price and pickup deadline.
  • An opt-in alert for customers who want to hear about it, with a limit of one per day.
  • A reserve button, so two people do not make the trip for the last loaf.
  • Offers that expire automatically at closing time.

Why it is useful: the shop has a chance to sell food that might otherwise go unsold, and customers get a reason to stop by during a quiet stretch. Surplus food marketplaces already exist, so the point of this idea is different: the list lives inside the shop’s own app, next to its loyalty card, and speaks to its own regulars. A real version would also need to respect local food safety and labeling rules.

Idea 2: A “Who Owns This VM?” Check-In

Who it helps: IT teams whose vSphere inventory includes virtual machines that nobody is quite willing to claim.

Core features:

  • A scheduled, read-only collection of VM names, power state, notes and vSphere tags.
  • A list of machines with no recorded owner or purpose.
  • A periodic prompt asking each owner to answer: still needed, not needed, or belongs to someone else.
  • A history of answers, plus an export for audits and cleanup meetings.

Why it is useful: unclaimed VMs can use storage and licenses, miss patch cycles and make every cleanup a guessing game. Asking owners a simple question on a regular schedule is easier than investigating years later. The app would never power off or delete anything. It would only collect answers so a person can decide. A well-chosen PowerCLI query is a sensible starting point for the inventory side.

Idea 3: A Seasonal Home Upkeep Reminder

Who it helps: homeowners and renters who remember the air filter only when something starts to sound wrong.

Core features:

  • A starter checklist by season, such as filters, smoke alarm tests, gutters and outdoor taps.
  • Reminders that count from the last time a job was actually done, not just from a fixed calendar date.
  • A note and photo on each task, such as the filter size or where the shutoff valve is.
  • A shared list, so everyone in the household can see what is finished.

Why it is useful: small jobs are easy to forget and can become expensive when they pile up. Keeping the sizes and photos with the reminder also saves a second trip to the hardware store. It would pair naturally with the household warranty vault from our earlier list.

Testing an Idea Before Building It

You can try each of these without writing an app. A shop could post day-end offers by hand for a couple of weeks. An IT team could send owners a short spreadsheet and see who replies. A household could share a simple checklist. If people keep using the rough version, you learn which features matter and which can wait. If they drop it, you have saved yourself the cost of building it.

That habit of starting small is how we like to approach projects such as The Coffee Shop, a customer app still in design, and Beam, an early prototype of a TV remote. If one of these ideas matches a problem you deal with, or you have a better one, I would like to hear about it.
