Getting Your Small Business App Into the App Stores: A Launch Checklist

By RMG LabsNovember 27, 20264 min read

Building the app is only part of the job. Before customers can download it, Apple and Google need to know who you are, check what the app does and, in some cases, see that real people have tested it. None of this is difficult, but several steps take days rather than minutes. Starting them early keeps a finished app from sitting on the shelf. Here is a plain-language checklist, based on the store rules as published in late November 2026. Rules and fees change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

1. Decide Whose Name Goes on the App

Open the developer accounts in your business’s name, using an email address the business controls. If a contractor publishes the app under their own account, moving it later is extra work. You can invite your developer to help while you remain the owner.

The type of account matters. With Apple, an individual or sole proprietor enrolls as an individual, and their personal legal name appears as the seller. An organization must be a legal entity (Apple does not accept trade names or DBAs), and its legal name appears as the seller. Google also offers personal and organization accounts, and says to choose an organization account for a business.

2. Gather Your Business Details

Organization accounts need a D-U-N-S number, a business identifier issued by Dun & Bradstreet. Google describes applying for one as free, and Apple says it is free in most places. Apple also asks organizations for a working public website on the business’s own domain, a work email on that domain, and someone with the legal authority to accept agreements. Check that your business name and address match across your records, and allow time for verification.

3. Budget for the Account Fees

  • Apple Developer Program: 99 US dollars per membership year, or the local equivalent (Apple’s membership details).
  • Google Play: a one-time registration fee of 25 US dollars (Play Console help).

These are only the store fees. Design, development, hosting, payment processing and ongoing maintenance are separate, so ask for them as separate lines in any quote.

4. Plan for Testing Time

If you use a personal Google Play account created after November 13, 2023, you must run a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for 14 days in a row before you can apply to publish publicly. Google then reviews that application, which it says usually takes seven days or less (testing requirements). Line up testers early. Even when no rule requires it, a short trial with staff and a few regular customers is a good way to find confusing screens.

5. Handle Privacy and Accounts

Publish a privacy policy on your website and link it in your store listings and inside the app. It should say plainly what you collect, how you use it and who else receives it, including any analytics or payment services the app relies on. Both stores also ask you to declare your data practices in their own forms.

If customers can create an account in your app, plan how they delete it. Apple requires that option inside the app. Google requires an in-app path and a web link where people can request deletion (account deletion rules). Build this from the start; it is harder to add at the end.

6. Prepare for Review

Apple reports that it reviews most submissions within a day or two (App Review), but a rejection adds another round. Before you submit:

  • Give reviewers a working demo login if any feature requires signing in, and keep your server running.
  • Remove placeholder text, test items and broken links.
  • Test on real phones, including with a poor connection.
  • Use screenshots that show the app as it actually looks and works.
  • Include a support contact that someone really checks.

Work Backward From Your Launch Date

If you want the app live for a busy season or a grand opening, start the accounts and verification first, then testing, then review, and leave spare time for fixes. Do not print the “download our app” signs until the listing is live. Afterward, put the Apple renewal date on your calendar and budget for updates as phones and store rules change.
