Beam and a Pool Chemical App: Two Fun Projects

By RMG LabsNovember 22, 20264 min read

Not every project at RMG Labs starts with a client request. Some start with a small annoyance at home and a thought of “surely there is an app for that.” Two of those are worth sharing: a remote control app that already exists as an early prototype, and a pool chemical app that is, for now, only an idea.

Beam: One Remote for Every TV

Anyone with more than one TV knows the routine. Each set has its own remote, the remotes migrate between rooms, and the one you need is always in another room. Beam is an attempt to fix that with the phone already in your hand. It finds the TVs on your Wi-Fi and controls them from a single app, so there is one remote layout to learn instead of four.

The current prototype is aimed at Roku, Samsung, Google TV and Fire TV sets. It offers the usual remote basics: a directional pad, playback controls, volume, channels and quick input switching. It keeps a saved list of your TVs with a status for each, lets you rename them, and can scan your network or accept an address typed in by hand. Setup tips for each brand appear right on the screen, because pairing a TV is where most remote apps lose people.

What Building It Teaches

A project like this is a good reminder that the hard part of a simple-looking app is rarely the buttons. Each TV maker speaks its own language, older models behave differently from newer ones, and a set that works perfectly in testing may need a different approach at the next house. That is why Beam is still described honestly as an early prototype that is being tested on more TV models, and why it is not in the app stores yet. There is a browser demo on the project page that uses sample TVs, so you can see the layout without it connecting to anything real.

The same lessons apply to business apps: start with one clear job, expect real-world quirks, and test on more than one device before calling something finished.

A Planned Idea: A Pool Chemical App

The second project has not been started. It is an idea only, and nothing described here exists yet.

Anyone who looks after a pool knows the cycle: test the water, squint at a colored strip or a drop-count kit, try to remember what the numbers were last week, and then decide what to add. The idea is a small app that makes that routine less of a guessing game. Possible features for a first version might include:

  • A simple log where you type in your test readings, such as chlorine and pH, with the date.
  • A history view so you can see whether a number is drifting rather than just where it is today.
  • Reminders for the regular tasks, like testing, cleaning a filter or checking the equipment.
  • Notes, so you can record what you added and what the weather was doing.

Anything involving how much of a chemical to add would need careful thought. Pool chemicals can be hazardous, and amounts depend on pool size, the product and the manufacturer’s label. If that ever becomes part of the app, the label and a pool professional’s advice would come first. A tracker that simply keeps your readings in one place is a useful and much safer starting point.

Why Share Projects That Are Not Finished?

Side projects are where ideas get tested cheaply. A prototype shows what is possible and what is awkward long before anyone commits real money to a larger build. If you have a small annoyance of your own, whether it is a household task, a hobby or a repeated chore at work, it may be a better app idea than you think.
